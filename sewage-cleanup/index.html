Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
