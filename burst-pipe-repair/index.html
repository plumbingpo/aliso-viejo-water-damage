Few water damage events are as common, or as potentially devastating to a property, as a pipe that bursts. A single burst pipe can release 4 to 8 gallons of water per minute, which is 240 to 480 gallons per hour flooding through your walls, ceilings and floors. Pipes burst from freezing, old plumbing, too much water pressure, corrosion or a physical hit, and in every case a fast emergency response is what keeps water from ruining flooring, structural materials, building systems and your belongings.

Following extraction, our structural drying process addresses all moisture-affected materials, including drywall, insulation, wood framing, subflooring and finished flooring. Why Burst Pipe Repair Is Not a DIY Job

The damage from a burst pipe tends to reach well past the wet areas in plain view. Water travels through wall cavities, along electrical conduits, through floor assemblies and between ceiling joists, saturating materials in places you may not discover for days or weeks. By then, mold may already be established in hidden spaces and structural materials may be significantly weakened. With moisture meters and thermal cameras, trained technicians follow the water's path and pinpoint every affected area right away.

Consumer tools cannot compare with the scale of professional drying and extraction equipment. Compared with a shop vacuum, truck-mounted extraction is hundreds of times faster, and compared with a residential dehumidifier, a commercial one removes 10 to 20 times as much moisture. With only 24 to 48 hours before mold starts, you need equipment this fast and this strong.

Burst Pipe Repair Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Replace old galvanized steel or polybutylene pipes before they fail unexpectedly
  • During freezing weather, keep faucets dripping and cabinet doors open so heat can circulate
  • Pressure higher than 80 PSI wears on pipes and their fittings, so test yours from time to time
  • Unheated spaces like garages, attics and crawl spaces need pipe insulation on exposed lines
  • Leave the heat on when you travel in winter so indoor pipes stay above freezing
  • Know where your main water shutoff valve is and how to operate it quickly

Warning Signs for Burst Pipe Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Wet spots, bulging or staining on walls or ceilings
  • A spray or steady drip coming off pipework you can see
  • Faucets and showers across the building suddenly running weak
  • Unexplained standing water along baseboards or in the middle of a room
  • Frost or ice on exposed pipes during cold weather
  • Hearing water move through the house while nothing is turned on
  • Faucets that give only a trickle, or nothing, during a freeze
