Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Aliso Viejo

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

Our Aliso Viejo crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the Machine DownStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
  • An overflow caused by an overloaded drum or by using too much detergent.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Mold Behind Appliances

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

In a home with an upstairs laundry, leak water passes through the floor and pools on the ceiling underneath. The drywall stains and sags, and the insulation above it stays wet.

Flooring and Subfloor

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Wall Bases and Trim

On a flooded kitchen or laundry floor, the baseboards and the lower edge of the drywall draw water upward. The wall behind the appliance is often the wettest because nobody could see it.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Whether mold has started and has to be removed before the area can be dried.
  • Whether the water was clean supply water or gray discharge that needs sanitizing.
  • The appliance involved, and if the leak was a slow seep or a fast flood.
  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.

How We Fix It

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

1

Pull and Inspect

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

2

Remove Standing Water

Carpet and floors are cleared of standing water with extraction equipment. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

3

Dry in Place

We set air movers and dehumidifiers and, where possible, dry cabinets and hardwood in place with directed airflow so they do not have to be torn out.

4

Sanitize and Take Final Readings

We apply antimicrobial treatment wherever discharge from the washer or the dishwasher made contact. We take final moisture readings before the appliance goes back so it is not set on a damp floor.
